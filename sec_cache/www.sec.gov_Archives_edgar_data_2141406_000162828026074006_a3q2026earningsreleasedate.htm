Accelevation Holdings Corp. Reports Strong Third Quarter 2026 with Revenue Growth of
66%, Bookings Growth of over 350%, and Robust Cash Generation
November 12, 2026
Third Quarter 2026 Business Highlights
•Revenue grew 65.9% year over year to $202.2 million.
•Bookings of $768.9 million, an increase of 364% year over year.
•Backlog of $1.7 billion, an increase of 329% and 51.0% over prior year and prior quarter,
respectively.
•Book-to-bill ratio of 3.8x.
•Net income was $3.2 million, a decline of $10.5 million; net income margin was 1.6%.
•Adjusted net income* was $20.9 million, a decline of 7.0%. 
•Adjusted EBITDA* increased by $8.9 million to $37.7 million; Adjusted EBITDA margin* was 18.7%.
•Operating cash flow increased to $62.5 million from an outflow of $0.6 million in the prior year.
•Subsequent to quarter end, the Company entered into a new $650 million senior secured credit
agreement with significant pricing improvement.
Full Year 2026 Guidance**
•Revenue in the range of $905-$915 million, representing 103% year-over-year growth at the
midpoint.
•Adjusted EBITDA** of $155-$165 million, representing 76% year-over-year growth at the midpoint.

MIAMISBURG, Ohio, November 12, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Accelevation, Inc. (NASDAQ: ACCV)
("Accelevation" or the "Company"), a leading designer, manufacturer and installer of customized
structural, electrical and mechanical systems for mission-critical infrastructure, reported financial results
for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Management Commentary
“At Accelevation, we design, manufacture, and install mission-critical infrastructure for customers who rely
on our speed and certainty of delivery, and our third quarter results reflect the strength of our operating
model and underlying markets,” said Michael Rubiera, Co-Founder and Chief Executive Officer of
Accelevation. "We are pleased with our first reported quarter as a public company. Our end markets are
accelerating, demand for our products is robust, and our ability to deliver customized solutions at scale for
our customers is driving strong market share gains and growth in our business. Our backlog, order pace,
and focus on productivity position us well to deliver strong results in the fourth quarter and beyond.”
“Our performance in the third quarter demonstrated the benefits of several strategic decisions we made
previously to position Accelevation for where the industry is headed,” Michael Rubiera continued. “Our
market is accelerating and we are seeing a shift toward modular, factory-built infrastructure. Based upon
the strong demand from our customers, we continue to make investments in scaling our modular
2
capabilities while also broadening our power product offering. We are expanding our addressable market
through product innovation, diversifying our customer base, and growing our business with existing blue-
chip customers to drive sustained, profitable growth.”
“We delivered strong growth, profitability, and cash generation in the quarter,” said Ken Krause, Chief
Financial Officer of Accelevation. “Revenue increased more than 60%, bookings grew more than 350%, 
backlog rose to approximately $1.7 billion, and Adjusted EBITDA margins approached 19% following a
weaker first half. Strong demand for our solutions continues to drive significant growth, while our focus on
execution translated into improved margins. Operating cash flow increased significantly to over $62
million, representing cash conversion well in excess of 100% of net income for the quarter.”
“Following quarter end, we used the primary proceeds from our IPO to reduce debt and closed $650
million in new credit facilities with improved pricing while significantly decreasing our pro forma net
leverage following the IPO,” Mr. Krause continued. “We believe our balance sheet is well positioned, with
our new credit facilities providing ample capacity. Our reduced leverage post-IPO and enhanced financial
flexibility allow us to continue investing in the business to support strong and growing demand across our
end markets.”
“As we introduce full-year 2026 guidance today we are well positioned to continue delivering robust
growth, an improving margin profile, and strong cash flow as we start the fourth quarter and look further
out into 2027,” concluded Mr. Krause.

Initial Public Offering
On October 1, 2026, after the end of the third quarter, Accelevation completed its initial public offering of
30,000,000 shares of Class A common stock at a public offering price of $18.00 per share. The offering
consisted of 10,000,000 shares sold by Accelevation and 20,000,000 shares sold by certain selling
stockholders affiliated with Olympus Partners. Accelevation’s Class A common stock began trading on the
Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol “ACCV” on September 30, 2026.
Accelevation received net proceeds of approximately $170.6 million after deducting underwriting
discounts and commissions and estimated offering expenses. It used the net proceeds to purchase newly
issued units of Accelevation Holdings LLC. Accelevation Holdings LLC applied those proceeds to repay
outstanding indebtedness. Accelevation did not receive any proceeds from the sale of shares by the
selling stockholders.
In connection with the initial public offering, Accelevation entered into new senior secured credit facilities
consisting of a $400 million revolving credit facility and a $250 million Term Loan A. Because the offering
closed after quarter end, third quarter financial results do not reflect the offering or the related
transactions.

About Accelevation Holdings Corp.
Accelevation is a leading designer, manufacturer and installer of customized structural, electrical and
mechanical systems for mission-critical infrastructure. Its vertically integrated model combines U.S.-based
manufacturing and nationwide field service capabilities to move customers faster from design through
deployment with innovative, factory-built solutions engineered for speed, scalability and certainty.
Accelevation solves complex needs at scale while investing in its communities, growing the skilled trades
and creating opportunities for people to build lasting careers and share in the value they create.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
Adjusted SG&A and Adjusted SG&A Margin
Adjusted SG&A is calculated as SG&A plus or minus (i) equity-based compensation, (ii) public company
readiness costs, (iii) acquisition-related costs, (iv) changes in the fair value of contingent consideration
liabilities, and (v) other non-recurring items. Adjusted SG&A Margin reflects Adjusted SG&A as a
percentage of Revenue.
Among other limitations, Adjusted SG&A and Adjusted SG&A Margin do not reflect all cash expenditures,
do not reflect certain recurring noncash charges, and do not reflect the impact of certain cash charges
resulting from matters the Company considers not to be indicative of ongoing operations.
The Company presents Adjusted SG&A and Adjusted SG&A Margin because it believes the metrics assist
investors and analysts in comparing performance across reporting periods on a consistent basis by
excluding items that the Company does not believe are indicative of core operating performance.
Management uses these metrics to compare SG&A expenses consistently over various periods.
Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin
Adjusted EBITDA is defined as net income (loss) adjusted for interest income and expense, provision for
income taxes, depreciation and amortization expense, equity-based compensation expense, public
company readiness costs, acquisition costs, sponsor fees and expenses, change in fair value of
acquisition earnout and asset impairment, as well as certain non-recurring items, including payments to
Olympus that ceased upon the IPO. Adjusted EBITDA Margin reflects Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage
of Revenue.
Among other limitations, Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin do not reflect the Company's
cash expenditures, or future requirements, for capital expenditures or contractual commitments, non-cash
charges for depreciation and amortization, and do not reflect the impact of certain cash charges resulting
from matters the Company considers not to be indicative of ongoing operations such as interest expense.
Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin also do not reflect income tax expense or benefit.
The Company believes that Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin are important metrics for
management and investors as they (i) adjust for the impact of items that the Company does not believe
are indicative of core operating results or the overall health of the Company and (ii) allow for consistent
comparison of operating results over time. In addition, the Company uses Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted
EBITDA Margin in evaluating management’s performance when determining incentive compensation and
to evaluate the effectiveness of its business strategies.
Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income Margin
Adjusted Net Income is calculated as net income (loss) plus or minus (i) amortization of intangibles, (ii)
equity-based compensation, (iii) sponsor fees and expenses, (iv) public company readiness costs, (v)
acquisition-related costs, (vi) changes in the fair value of contingent consideration liabilities, (vii) asset
impairments, (viii) other non-recurring items, and (ix) tax impact of adjustments. Adjusted Net Income
Margin reflects Adjusted Net Income as a percentage of Revenue.
Among other limitations, Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income Margin do not reflect all cash
expenditures, or future requirements, for capital expenditures or contractual commitments, do not reflect
certain recurring noncash charges, and do not reflect the impact of certain cash charges resulting from
matters the Company considers not to be indicative of ongoing operations.
The Company presents Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income Margin because it believes the
metrics assist investors and analysts in comparing performance across reporting periods on a consistent
basis by excluding items that the Company does not believe are indicative of core operating performance.

Free Cash Flow
The Company defines Free Cash Flow as Net Cash Provided by Operating Activities reduced by
Purchases of Property and Equipment. Management uses Free Cash Flow to provide insight into liquidity, 
cash-generating capability, as well as demonstrate its ability to fund future growth. Free Cash Flow should
be considered in addition to, rather than as a substitute for, Net Cash Provided by Operating Activities as
a measure of liquidity. Additionally, the Company's definition of Free Cash Flow is limited, in that it does
not represent residual cash flows available for discretionary expenditures, due to the fact that the
measure does not deduct the payments required for debt service and other contractual obligations or
payments made for business acquisitions. Therefore, management believes it is important to view Free
Cash Flow as a measure that provides supplemental information to the consolidated statements of cash
flows.

Performance Metrics
The Company utilizes certain performance metrics (defined below) to provide an indication of trends in
operating results, including future business, and to provide a useful indicator of the volume of our
business over time. Measuring backlog, bookings, and book-to-bill ratio involves the use of judgments,
and there are no independent standards or requirements governing the calculation of these metrics.
13
These metrics may be impacted by, among other factors, the types of services and systems sold, contract
duration, the pace of customer spending, actual volumes of services and systems delivered as compared
to the volumes anticipated at the time of sale, and contract modifications over the lifetime of a contract.
Information regarding these performance metrics is not comparable to, nor should it be substituted for, an
analysis of reported revenues.
Backlog consists of the remaining unrecognized revenue on executed contracts and purchase orders, as
well as letters of intent and notices to proceed with respect to purchase orders received in writing. The
Company utilizes backlog to provide additional insights regarding trends in future revenue and market
penetration.
Bookings is defined as the aggregate dollar value of executed customer contracts and purchase orders,
as well as letters of intent and notices to proceed with respect to purchase orders received in writing,
during a given period.
Book-to-bill ratio is defined as bookings divided by revenue for the applicable period.
