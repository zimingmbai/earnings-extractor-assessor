FIRST BUSINESS BANK REPORTS RECORD THIRD QUARTER EARNINGS

-- Core deposit growth outpaces robust loan growth; positive operating leverage drives further tangible book value growth --

 

MADISON, Wis., October 29, 2026 (BUSINESS WIRE) -- First Business Financial Services, Inc. (the “Company”, the “Bank”, or “First Business Bank”) (Nasdaq: FBIZ) reported quarterly net income available to common shareholders of $16.4 million, or earnings per share ("EPS") of $1.95. This compares to net income available to common shareholders of $15.4 million, or $1.84 per share, in the second quarter of 2026 and $14.2 million, or $1.70 per share, in the third quarter of 2025.

 

“Our record third quarter results reflect strong execution across First Business Bank’s relationship-based model,” said Dave Seiler, President and Chief Executive Officer. “Annualized loan growth of approximately 10% was more than fully funded by 14% annualized core deposit growth, while a strong net interest margin and disciplined expense management drove record pre-tax, pre-provision earnings. Through the first nine months of 2026, earnings per share increased 20%, pre-tax, pre-provision earnings increased 14%, and tangible book value per share grew 13% from the prior year.”

“We expect expenses to increase from the third-quarter level as we make planned investments in talent, marketing, technology, and infrastructure to support continued growth. These investments are integral to our long-term model of approximately 10% annual balance sheet growth and positive annual operating leverage. We believe consistent execution of our strategic plan, together with disciplined credit and capital management, will continue to compound earnings and tangible book value per share and support strong long-term profitability.”

 

 

Quarterly Highlights

 

•
Record Earnings and Pre-Tax, Pre-Provision ("PTPP") Growth. Net income available to common shareholders reached a record $16.4 million, or $1.95 per diluted share. PTPP adjusted earnings grew to a record $21.3 million, up 7.6% and 13.1% from the linked and prior-year quarters, respectively.
•
Relationship-Based Deposit Growth Funded Loan Expansion. Core deposits increased by $101.3 million, or 14.1% annualized, from the linked quarter and $386.8 million, or 14.9%, from the prior-year quarter, outpacing loan growth in both periods. Core deposits improved to 76.7% of total bank funding, allowing wholesale funding to decline by $103.1 million.
•
Continued Loan Growth. Loans increased $88.6 million, or 9.9% annualized, from the linked quarter and $340.2 million, or 10.2%, from the third quarter of 2025.
•
Strong Net Interest Margin. Net interest margin was 3.80%, compared to 3.78% for the linked quarter and 3.68% for the prior-year quarter. The modest linked improvement was primarily driven by favorable loan yields and an improved funding mix. Net interest margin was 3.72% and 3.68% for the first nine months of 2026 and 2025, respectively. Elevated fees in lieu of interest continued to support the year-over-year comparison.
•
SBA Exit Improves Operating Efficiency. Operating expenses declined $706,000, or 2.6%, from the linked quarter, primarily due to lower SBA-related compensation costs. The Company expects annual operating leverage to remain modest but positive.
•
Strong Value Creation. Through the first nine months of 2026, operating revenue increased 9.8% while operating expense increased 6.8%, driving a 14.2% increase in pre-tax, pre-provision earnings and 20% EPS growth. Tangible book value per share increased 10.5% annualized from the linked quarter and 13.4% from the prior year.

Third Quarter 2026 Compared to Second Quarter 2026

Net interest income increased $936,000, or 2.5%, to $39.1 million.

•
Net interest income increased as average loans and leases receivable grew by $66.0 million, or 7.4% annualized during the third quarter. This increase was partially offset by a $193,000 decrease in prepayment fees.
•
The yield on average interest-earning assets increased 2 basis points to 6.47% from 6.45%.
•
The rate paid for average core deposits was stable at 2.41% compared to 2.40%, and the rate paid on average total bank funding was stable at 2.76% compared to 2.75%. Total bank funding includes total deposits and Federal Home Loan Bank (“FHLB”) advances.
•
Net interest margin increased to 3.80% from 3.78% in the linked quarter. The linked-quarter increase reflected more favorable loan yields and deposit mix, including growth in non-interest-bearing demand deposits, as well as a modest favorable day-count impact. These benefits more than offset lower prepayment and asset-based loan fees. While net interest margin is expected to remain above the Company’s long-term target range of 3.60% to 3.65% during the fourth quarter, it is expected to moderate from the third-quarter level as fee income normalizes. The Company continues to view 3.60% to 3.65% as an appropriate long-term range.

The Bank reported provision for credit losses of $1.9 million compared to $2.1 million in the linked quarter. Compared to the linked quarter, the provision for credit losses was lower primarily due to a smaller increase in quantitative factors and lower loan growth, partially offset by higher net charge-offs. See the Provision for Credit Loss breakdown table below for more detail.

Non-interest income decreased $140,000, or 1.6%, to $8.4 million.

•
Commercial loan swap fee income increased $393,000, or 242.6%, to $555,000. Swap fee income varies from period to period based on loan activity and the interest rate environment.
•
Other non-interest income decreased $494,000 to $1.0 million, primarily due to a decrease in limited partnership investment income.

Non-interest expense decreased $1.7 million, or 6.3%, to $26.1 million, while operating expense decreased $706,000, or 2.6%, to $26.2 million. Third-quarter operating expense primarily benefited from lower compensation expense due to our exit from out-of-market SBA 7(a) lending. We expect the operating expense run rate to increase from the third-quarter level, primarily reflecting investments in talent, technology, and marketing initiatives. These investments are intended to expand the Company’s capacity for sustained growth. While quarterly comparisons may vary, the company continues to expect modest positive operating leverage annually.

•
Compensation expense was $17.8 million, decreasing by $693,000, or 3.8% from the linked quarter. The decrease was primarily driven by lower salaries and benefits expense and linked quarter severance expense related to the Company's strategic exit from out of market SBA 7(a) lending activities. This decrease was partially offset by higher annual cash bonus accruals reflecting above-target Company performance. Average full-time equivalents ("FTEs") for the third quarter were 352, compared to 366 in the linked quarter. Excluding SBA employees, average FTEs were 351 for the third quarter compared to 354 in the linked quarter.
•
Other non-interest expense decreased $656,000 to $1.2 million, primarily due to an SBA recourse provision benefit and changes in carrying values on historic tax credit investments.
•
Professional fees expense decreased $204,000, or 13.7%, to $1.3 million, primarily reflecting annual stock awards issued to directors in the second quarter.
•
FDIC insurance expense decreased $123,000, or 15.0%, to $696,000, due to improved leverage and brokered deposit ratios.
•
Marketing expense decreased $105,000, or 12.5%, to $735,000, due to timing of marketing programs and a reduction in sponsorship activity.

Income tax expense increased $1.7 million to $2.9 million. The effective tax rate was 14.8% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to 7.2% for the linked quarter. The change in tax expense primarily reflects the release of a state deferred tax valuation allowance in the linked quarter and higher pre-tax income in the current quarter. The Company expects to report a full year 2026 effective tax rate between 13% and 15%.

Total gross period-end loans and leases receivable increased $88.6 million, or 9.9% annualized, to $3.677 billion. The average rate earned on average loans and leases receivable was 6.79%, up from 6.76% in the linked quarter.

•
CRE loans increased $77.2 million, or 14.3% annualized, to $2.239 billion, primarily due to growth in the Wisconsin markets.
•
C&I loans increased $9.4 million, or 2.7% annualized, to $1.390 billion, primarily due to growth in Floorplan Financing.

Total period-end core deposits increased $101.3 million, or 14.1% annualized, to $2.979 billion, reflecting broad-based growth across our markets. The average rate paid was stable at 2.41% compared to 2.40% in the linked quarter.

Period-end wholesale funding, including FHLB advances and brokered deposits, decreased $103.1 million, or 10.24%, to $903.3 million. Wholesale funding continues to support interest rate risk management through match-funding of fixed-rate assets, enhancing funding flexibility, and stabilizing net interest margin.

•
Wholesale deposits increased $56.3 million to $770.8 million. The average rate paid on wholesale deposits increased four basis points to 4.07% and the weighted average original maturity increased to 4.0 years from 3.3 years.
•
FHLB advances decreased $159.4 million to $132.5 million. The average rate paid on FHLB advances increased two basis points to 3.55% and the weighted average original maturity remained flat at 6.0 years.

Nonperforming assets increased $2.2 million to $40.2 million, or 0.91% of total assets, compared with 0.86% in the linked quarter, primarily reflecting a new non-accrual multifamily loan associated with the previously disclosed Wisconsin relationship. This increase was partially offset by the charge-off of fully reserved C&I non-accrual loans. The Wisconsin relationship represented $22.7 million of non-accrual loans, collateralized by commercial real estate, with $416,000 of reserves at September 30, 2026. Year-to-date net charge-offs remained stable at 0.22% of average loans compared with 0.23% in the prior-year period.

The allowance for credit losses, including the unfunded credit commitments reserve, decreased $668,000, or 1.7%, primarily due to charge-offs of previously reserved loans and leases and a decrease in qualitative factors partially offset by increases in general reserves primarily due to loan growth and a modest decline in the economic outlook in our model forecast. The allowance for credit losses, including unfunded credit commitment reserves, as a percent of total gross loans and leases was 1.06%, compared to 1.10% in the linked quarter.
