PureCycle Technologies Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

 

•
Branded programs shipping, under purchase order, in contract negotiation, or final qualification are expected to reach approximately 45-55 million pounds per year at full ramp, which would approach the 40 to 50% utilization range
•
P&G products are on shelves; railcar shipments began in October to a large customer for hot lids; and resin is qualified for shampoo closures at a large global cosmetics company with shipments expected in Q4
•
Ironton produced 5.2 million pounds of PureFive® resin in Q3, up 14% sequentially; operational issues in Q3 limited production and were resolved; production is now in line with current commercial demand
•
Thailand construction permit issued; final terms of the project financing under negotiation
•
Third quarter average monthly operations spending below the guided range

ORLANDO, FLA., November 4, 2026 — PureCycle Technologies, Inc. (Nasdaq: PCT) (“PureCycle” or the “Company”), a U.S.-based company revolutionizing plastic recycling, today announced results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Third Quarter 2026 Highlights

Commercial

•
Railcar shipments started in October to a large customer for hot lids, representing a program of approximately 7–8 million pounds of expected annual volume once fully ramped
•
Procter & Gamble (P&G) products are on shelves, including Downy and Tide caps, with first Tide deliveries in October; Gain caps are a new application and another brand color the Company has matched; Vicks ZzzQuil lids are in commercial production
•
Resin shipments to a large global cosmetics company are expected to begin in Q4 for shampoo closures with 100% recycled content, and a large global personal care company has approved a white grade for deodorant sticks, with the final trial this month
•
Two major quick-service restaurant cold-cup programs are qualified; launches planned for 2026 moved to 2027 following the New Jersey exemption extension; term sheet conditions are agreed upon, and the supply agreement is under negotiation
•
New Jersey's extension of its food-contact exemption shifted about 15 food-contact programs, approximately 50 million pounds of expected annual volume once fully ramped, into 2027 and in many cases beyond. The Company has moved its primary sales and marketing effort to non-food applications, where the January 2027 compliance date still applies, with initial positive results in October

 

•
Q3 revenue of $2.3 million primarily reflects operational issues in the quarter and the New Jersey delays; strong branded customer and purchase-order momentum in October

Operations

•
Ironton produced 5.2 million pounds of PureFive® resin in Q3, up 14% from Q2
•
Operational issues in Q3 limited production and were resolved
•
Production is now in line with current commercial demand; as programs convert, the priority is supplying each customer consistently to its specification, so production is designed to rise with shipments
•
On-site compounding transitioned to 24/7 availability and is accelerating many customer programs, with more than 20 grades formulated in-house to meet customers’ specifications
•
The Company continued to make incremental improvements at Ironton to reduce costs and raise operating efficiency
•
Long-term feedstock agreement with WM, the largest recycler in North America, for up to 25 million pounds per year of waste polypropylene bales over three years beginning November 2026; the two companies are also continuing to work together on circularity programs with brands

Growth

•
The construction permit for the Rayong site was issued in September; groundbreaking will be scheduled once the timing of financial close is clear; total project cost estimate remains in the $250 million range
•
Final terms and conditions of the Thailand project financing are under negotiation
•
The Company has invested more than $85 million in equipment and engineering for the Rayong, Thailand project to date
•
Growth projects have reached internal milestones; discretionary spending is on hold until commercial progress at Ironton supports further investment

Finance

•
Total liquidity of $193 million at quarter-end, consisting of cash and cash equivalents, marketable securities and restricted cash, plus the undrawn $200 million revolving credit facility, now extended to September 30, 2028, for total available liquidity of $393 million
•
Monthly operations spending of $7.8 million in Q3, excluding materials purchases of approximately $2.7 million per month, below the Company's previously guided $8.0 to $9.0 million range
•
New cost reduction measures taking effect in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027: more reliance on in-house maintenance and less on contractors, certain roles consolidated and less reliance on temporary labor, outside services brought in-house or set to a routine level, logistics and storage sized to actual volumes, and on-site nitrogen supply in 2027
•
Project and growth spending is expected to be directionally lower in 2027 than in 2026, with a 2027 project spend range to be provided with fourth-quarter results
•
Net income of $6.9 million, including a $58.6 million non-cash gain on the change in fair value of warrants; Adjusted EBITDA of $(28.0) million, improved $0.6 million year over year

Management Commentary

“The third quarter fell short of our plan. Operational issues in Q3 limited production, but we resolved them. New Jersey's extension of the food-contact exemption was a significant challenge, moving about 15 food-contact programs, roughly 50 million pounds of expected annual volume once fully ramped, out of the fourth quarter. Some of those brands are continuing on their own timetable and others have deferred. Two quick-service restaurant cup programs are progressing toward a 2027 ramp, and we have moved our primary sales and

 

marketing effort to non-food applications already in the pipeline, with initial positive results in October,” said Dustin Olson, Chief Executive Officer of PureCycle Technologies.

Olson continued, “Despite the NJ delay, the branded sales ramp is underway. This is a credit to our diverse pipeline and the flexibility of our product. P&G products are on shelves, railcars are moving to a large customer, a large global cosmetics company is expected to begin shipping this quarter, and a large global personal care company has approved our white grade. Including programs in contract negotiation or final qualification, we expect to approach the 40 to 50% utilization range once these programs fully ramp. On site compounding allows us to deliver the grade each customer specifies, which shortens the sales process. More of what decides our success is now within our control, and we are confident in the path ahead.”

“We ended the quarter with $193 million of liquidity and an undrawn $200 million revolver that matures in September 2028, and we are implementing cost efficiencies across the organization,” said Donald Carpenter, Chief Financial Officer of PureCycle Technologies. “Monthly operations spending was $7.8 million, below our guided range, and we will continue reducing costs into 2027. For several years our spending was sized to the challenges of learning to run a first-of-its-kind commercial plant, and the results of our cost reduction efforts should show in monthly operations spending. The Thailand incentives are in place, the construction permit was issued in September, significant capital has been invested, and we are negotiating the final terms of the project financing. We’ve reached internal milestones on growth projects, and discretionary spending is on hold while Ironton ramps.”

Board Update

Separately today, the Company announced the appointment of Fernando Musa as Executive Chairman of the Board of Directors, effective November 3, 2026. Mr. Musa has served on PureCycle’s Board since 2021 and was its Lead Independent Director from 2021 to 2022. He served as Global Chief Executive Officer of Braskem S.A. from 2016 to 2019 and as Chief Executive Officer of Braskem America from 2012 to 2016, and he has advised private equity firms since 2020. His experience scaling polypropylene production and selling it to brands is directly relevant to the Ironton ramp. As Executive Chairman he will provide insight to the Company’s commercial and finance teams and assist management in executing its strategic objectives. Dan Coombs has stepped down as Chairman and will continue as a member of the Board. Dustin Olson continues as Chief Executive Officer.

Financial Update

Financial Results

Net income for Q3 2026 was $6.9 million, which included a $58.6 million non-cash gain on the change in fair value of warrants, compared to a net loss of $28.4 million in Q3 2025. Operating loss in Q3 2026 was $37.5 million compared to $38.9 million in Q3 2025. Adjusted EBITDA was $(28.0) million compared to $(28.6) million in Q3 2025, an improvement of $0.6 million.

Revenue was $2.3 million in Q3 2026, compared to $4.5 million in Q2 2026 and $2.4 million in Q3 2025, primarily reflecting operational issues in the quarter and the New Jersey delays. PureFive® production was 5.2 million pounds in Q3 2026 compared to 4.5 million pounds in Q2 2026 and 7.3 million pounds in Q3 2025.

Cash and Liquidity

PureCycle ended Q3 2026 with total liquidity of $193 million, which includes $109 million in cash and cash equivalents, $71.4 million invested in marketable securities and $12.6 million in restricted cash, compared to $236.9 million at the end of Q2 2026. Including the undrawn $200 million revolving credit facility, total available liquidity was $393 million at the end of Q3.

 

The Company identified cost reductions, with new measures in effect in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, and expects to continue reducing costs into 2027. The equipment financing payments concluded in September 2026.

Project Spend

Project spend in Q3 2026 totaled $13.0 million, bringing project spend for the first nine months of 2026 to $47.6 million. The Company expects fourth-quarter project spend of approximately $4 million, mainly committed costs of its other projects, and full-year project spend of approximately $51 million, compared with the full-year range of $45 to $50 million provided in August. Project and growth spending is expected to be directionally lower in 2027 than in 2026. Expected project spend in Q4 2026 and 2027 depends on the timing of the Thailand financing, and the Company will provide a 2027 range with its fourth-quarter results.

Capital Structure

The Company's capital structure is unchanged from the June 2026 refinancing other than the August 2026 extension of the maturity of its $200 million revolving credit facility to September 30, 2028. That facility remains undrawn.

Fourth-quarter 2026 debt service is expected to be approximately $9.4 million, mainly the December 1, 2026 revenue bond payment. The first coupon on the 4.75% senior convertible notes is payable in January 2027. Equipment financing payments concluded in September 2026.

The Company's available capital resources are cash, marketable securities and the undrawn $200 million revolving credit facility. Potential future sources of capital, which the Company does not assume in its liquidity planning, are approximately $76 million of revenue bonds held by the Company, equipment financing on owned PreP equipment ($67.3 million original cost) and approximately $272 million of proceeds if the warrants expiring in March 2027 are exercised at $11.50.

About PureCycle Technologies

PureCycle Technologies LLC, a subsidiary of PureCycle Technologies, Inc., holds a global license for the only patented dissolution recycling technology, developed by The Procter & Gamble Company (P&G), that is designed to transform polypropylene plastic waste (designated as #5 plastic) into a continuously renewable resource. The unique purification process removes color, odor, and other impurities from #5 plastic waste resulting in our PureFive® resin that can be recycled and reused multiple times, changing our relationship with plastic. www.purecycle.com

Information Regarding Non-GAAP Financial Measures

The Company uses certain financial measures that are not calculated in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the U.S. (“GAAP”) to supplement its financial statements. These non-GAAP financial measures provide additional information to investors to facilitate comparisons of past and present operating results, identify trends in our underlying operating performance, and offer greater transparency on how the Company evaluates its business activities. These measures are integral to the Company’s process for budgeting, managing operations, making strategic decisions and evaluating its performance.

The Company’s primary non-GAAP financial measures are EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA. The Company defines EBITDA as net income before interest expense, interest income, taxes and depreciation and amortization. Adjusted EBITDA is defined as EBITDA further adjusted to exclude certain non-cash items and other items that are not indicative of the Company’s core operating activities. These may include equity-based compensation expense and changes in the fair value of warrants and put options, and other financial items. The Company believes Adjusted EBITDA is valuable for investors and analysts as it provides additional insight into the Company’s operational performance, excluding the impacts of certain financing, investing, and other non-operational activities. This measure helps in comparing the Company’s current operating results with prior periods and with those of other companies in the Company’s industry. It is also used internally for allocating resources efficiently, assessing strategic decisions, and evaluating the performance of the Company’s management team.

There are limitations to Adjusted EBITDA, including its exclusion of cash expenditures, future requirements for capital expenditures and contractual commitments, and changes in the Company’s cash requirements for working capital needs. Adjusted EBITDA also omits significant interest expense and related cash requirements for interest and payments. While depreciation and amortization are non-cash charges, the associated assets will often need to be replaced in the future, and Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect the cash required for such replacements. Additionally, Adjusted EBITDA does not account for income or other taxes or necessary cash tax payments.

Investors should use caution when comparing the Company’s non-GAAP measure to similar metrics used by other companies, as definitions can vary. Neither EBITDA nor Adjusted EBITDA should be considered in isolation or as a substitute for GAAP financial measures. In presenting EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA, the Company aims to provide investors with an additional tool for assessing the operational performance of the Company’s business. It serves as a useful complement to the Company’s GAAP results, offering a more comprehensive understanding of the Company’s financial health and operational efficiencies.
