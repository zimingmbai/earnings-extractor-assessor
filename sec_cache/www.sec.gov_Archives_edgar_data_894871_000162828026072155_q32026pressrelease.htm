Paradium.AI Reports Q3 2026 Results

Quarterly Performance Impacted by Continued Traffic Headwinds; Monetization and Technology Initiatives Gain Momentum, Boosting Yield and Content Output

NEW YORK – November 9, 2026 – Paradium.AI, Inc., formerly known as The Arena Group Holdings, Inc., (NYSE American: PAAI) (“Paradium.AI” or “Paradium” or “the Company”), a technology leader in IP, data and commerce that builds and operates a unified, AI-powered ecosystem designed to empower creators, media entrepreneurs, publishers and brands, today announced financial results for the three months ended September 30, 2026 (“Q3 2026”).
Financial Highlights for Q3 2026:
•Q3 2026 revenue was $20.5 million, compared to $29.8 million in Q3 2025, a reduction of 31.2%.
•Gross margin was 31.7% in Q3 2026, compared to 50.0% in Q3 2025.
•Net loss in Q3 2026 was $3.8 million, or -18.5% of revenue, compared to net income of $6.9 million, or 23.2% of revenue in Q3 2025.
•Adjusted EBITDA for Q3 2026 was $2.4 million compared to Adjusted EBITDA of $11.9 million in Q3 2025. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 11.7% in Q3 2026 compared to 39.9%, in Q3 2025.
•Cash balance of $10.1 million at September 30, 2026, maintaining consistency with year-end 2025 levels.

Third-quarter results were driven by lower referral traffic, including from Google Discover, reflecting the challenging distribution environment affecting digital publishers across the industry. These traffic reductions drove a decline in advertising revenue, which in turn, compressed margins as platform costs are largely fixed in the near-term. Quarterly loss also included the $1.3 million reversal of an employee retention tax credit asset that was disallowed, a decision the Company plans to appeal, and the timing of recognition of annual incentive compensation expenses of $1.0 million. Paradium remains focused on executing its strategic shift to reduce dependence on referral traffic sources by developing durable revenue streams derived from the Company’s growing portfolio of AI and technology based tools and products.

Operational Highlights:
•Monetization Improvements: Blended revenue per page view (RPM) rose 15% month over month in September 2026 from August 2026, driven by monetization initiatives deployed following experiments conducted in the first half of 2026.
•Increased Article Volume: New article production increased 15% over Q2 2026, expanding sequentially each month in Q3 2026, enabled by Paradium’s AI technologies, InfoSentience (acquired by the Company in August 2026) and the recently launched Cutter Studios.
•Licensing Co-Brand: Announced Trust·Respect·Love by Cesar Millan co-brand with Xcel Brands, combining celebrity expert credibility with the reach of PetHelpful, Parade, and Cesar Millan’s personal platforms and the content amplification of Cutter Studios to drive reach.
•CMS Improvements: Launched purpose-built, AI-enabled tool for content creation, editing and management in Paradium’s CMS in October, increasing the number of words reviewed in evergreen content by over 100%, providing deep, meaningful updates and in turn, increasing pageviews by approximately 50% within three weeks of updates.
•Agentic Buying: Implemented agentic buying campaigns in collaboration with pubX, creating opportunities tailored to brand specific audiences and environments for previously untapped

advertising spend, resulting in up to a 78% reduction in campaign trafficking time, taking only 5 hours and 37 minutes from discovery to launch, compared to the manual 2-3 business days.

“We continue to see tremendous opportunity in the age of AI, and we’re all in,” said Paul Edmondson, CEO of Paradium.AI. “While retaining referral traffic remains part of our focus, our real momentum is powered by tools like Cutter Studios and InfoSentience, which are already increasing output and efficiency. These tools, along with our recent CMS updates and upcoming launch of Tablation, our AI task management system built for content management and development, enable us to rapidly adapt to the future of modern publishing.”

We report our financial results in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States of America (“GAAP”); however, management believes that certain non-GAAP financial measures provide users of our financial information with useful supplemental information that enables a better comparison of our performance across periods. We believe Adjusted EBITDA provides visibility to the underlying continuing operating performance by excluding the impact of certain items that are noncash in nature or not related to our core business operations. We calculate Adjusted EBITDA as net (loss) income as adjusted for income from discontinued operations, interest expense (net), income taxes, and

depreciation and amortization. We further adjust for stock-based compensation and other special items that do not reflect our ongoing core operational performance, such as impairment costs, third-party vendor or professional settlement fees, liquidated damages, and government tax incentive credits. Our non-GAAP measure may not be comparable to similarly titled measures used by other companies, have limitations as an analytical tool, and should not be considered in isolation, or as a substitute for analysis of our operating results as reported under GAAP. Additionally, we do not consider our non-GAAP measure as superior to, or a substitute for, the equivalent measure calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP. Some of the limitations are that our non-GAAP measure:
●does not reflect interest expense and financing fees, or the cash required to service our debt, which reduces cash available to us;
●does not reflect income tax provision or benefit, which is a noncash income or expense;
●does not reflect depreciation and amortization expense and, although this is a noncash expense, the assets being depreciated may have to be replaced in the future, increasing our cash requirements;
●does not reflect stock-based compensation and, therefore, does not include all of our compensation costs;
●does not reflect the change in valuation of contingent consideration, and, although this is a noncash income or expense, the change in the valuations each reporting period are not impacted by our actual business operations but is instead strongly tied to the change in the market value of our common stock;
●does not reflect liquidated damages and, therefore, does not include future cash requirements if we repay the liquidated damages in cash instead of shares of our common stock (which the investor would need to agree to);
●does not reflect any losses from the impairment of assets, which is a noncash operating expense;
●does not reflect any losses from the sale of assets, which is a noncash operating expense;
●does not reflect the employee retention credits recorded by us for payroll related tax credits under the CARES Act;
●does not reflect payments related to employee restructuring charges for our former executives;
●does not reflect the professional and vendor fees incurred by us for services provided by consultants, accountants, lawyers, and other vendors, which services were related to certain types of events that are not reflective of our business operations;
●may not reflect proper non-direct cost allocations; and
●does not reflect acquisition-related fair value adjustments.
